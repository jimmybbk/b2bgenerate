Cold Email Outreach

B2BGenerate · Guides

What happens in the 71 hours after you hit send - the secret life of a cold email.

To you, each piece of email outreach is just a couple of sentences punched out of a dashboard and to the web it is just a faceless, nameless, non-reputationed character trying to waltz through six security checkpoints into the brain of someone who has just met their new best friend. Most never make it through the front door and a rare few manage to escape into a meeting which is worth more than they were paid to write.

But we live on these journeys, so we decided to tell one - truthfully - right through, the whole nine yards from outgoing sales email being 'sent' through to a human answering it (if it is lucky), and by the end, you just will not see your emails the same.

Prologue: The Two Months Nobody Sees

And while we are at it: that email exists before the minute it leaves your server! Weeks ago the sending domain was 'registered' with Google (the SPF and DKIM set up for you) and 'warming' started, as Google (and other such email platforms) really have strict criteria and do not treat the world as they do their own - they like to know that you are not just sending spam, so they send a few (like five every few days) emails to actual email boxes and reply, so it gets learned as a normal business email not a spam cannon. And this is not just paranoia, it is explicitly spelled out in their official documentation that should you receive complaints from users of more than 0.3% of your emails you are sending (and it is better to keep it to less than 0.1%) then you get bounced back - no 'second chances' - the moment you cross the threshold, you never ever get to get back on, because once you are considered a 'bulk sender' to Gmail (5,000+ emails a day) you are there for your lifetime. If you think your email is going to be able to fly under the radar and you are in luck then, Red Sift does a nice breakdown on the rules, which I would say - you are going for the 'slow and steady wins race' option.

Checkpoint Zero: The Right to Be There

Before our email earns the right to be sent there was a target - a warehouse operations manager at a fairly large logistics company with a specific set of turnover figures. Then there were the writing parts (a sentence of credentials, two lines of benefit, one line of favour asked), but then a compliance check - the inbox is a privilege subject to rules. The FTC's CAN-SPAM rule says headers must be verifiable, subject line not false or misleading and an unsubscribe; Australia's Spam Act requires consent and identification by the sender; Europe plays by the rules of legitimacy under GDPR. If the cold email does not have any right to be inside that inbox everything you have read above is wasted effort.

Hour Zero: Tuesday, 9:47 AM Local Time

Tuesday 9:47am is no coincidence, we know that the best time to send email is on a Wednesday but it is the morning after the Monday triage and before the Thursday Friday focus. 9:47am is no accident either it is the time people check email on their way in, post coffee, pre schedule overload. We know this from the benchmark data.

Checkpoint One: The Bouncers

The recipient's mail server will check its SPF settings in seconds. It will verify whether or not the sending domain is allowed to send using that server through SPF, the DKIM signature and whether it has DMARC alignment. If any of the above is not met, then your message will either not make it to your recipient, be rejected (no bounce message) or sent to quarantine or junk without a trace. Most Cold Mails which fail simply do not make it to the inbox and are therefore not "marked as spam". If users complain that "cold email does not work" what they generally mean is that their emails did not make it past the bouncers.

Checkpoint Two: Looking Like a Letter

So once through our authentication we send the email for a second set of gatekeepers - the spam-filters. These are trained via machine learning on billions of emails and are looking for anything that screams "marketing" including lots of HTML, images in the signature file, affiliate marketing links, subject lines about money etc. Therefore genuine marketing emails tend to look almost unexcitingly simple (HubSpot research indicates that simple text-only emails do better than designed HTML as they just look like a human actually wrote it) - so ours is a nine-line text-only message with a single link - it looks like a letter than a pamphlet and so is allowed through.

Hour Zero, Plus One Minute: The Three-Second Scan

It arrives in the in-box (the one before Promotions - the emails' tomb), between an inquiry from a client and an invoice from one of the client's companies. The email will receive three seconds of attention. Backlinko's analysis of 12 million outreach emails shows generic emails often go unanswered (and the ones that do not do not do so in great numbers), while ones with a bit more personalisation have a 32.7% increased response rate. The opening line of this email refers to an application for planning permission submitted by the recipient's firm last month. Specific, verifiable and relevant. All three checks passed.

Day One, Evening: Silence

Nothing! That is perfectly normal. That is the part everyone gives up on. The Backlinko survey showed that only 8.5% of emails ever get a reply - so it would be more accurate to say most get deleted straight away! Not replying is not necessarily turning you down - most often than not it is just that person has a full inbox at the beginning of the week. You just have to accept your e-mail is going in there right at the bottom of the list and just hope the person has one of the most undervalued traits of people who are good at outreach - patience!

Day Three: The Follow-Up

At 8:52 AM Thursday, we received the second email; two short sentences, no guilt, but a piece of new information. This one simple action would be one of the highest-use pieces of outreach in our campaign. Backlinko's research found that just one follow-up message will raise your response rate by approximately 65.8%, and further follow-ups will have an even greater impact. According to this research for the RAIN Group, it takes on average eight touches of a prospect by a sales team before getting their first meeting, so we had just had our fourth in a potential eight. The statistics make it clear that we should continue with some level of politeness than persistence.

Day Five: The Evening Read

9:40 PM Thursday. They scroll until it hits the top of the screen, three seconds scanning, thirty seconds reading it carefully. The product he is offering is a real pain point for their warehouse by the end of the quarter and his asking is not much: a simple fifteen-minute call at either 8:30 AM or 3:30 PM to talk. They star. And on any cold email, that is a door half ajar.

Hour 71: The Reply

On Monday at 10.15 we will get their response: "Happy to take a call - Tuesday afternoon work?" Now this is the big question, what are the chances? B2B benchmarks give open rates of around 42% for cold campaigns, reply rates of 4.8%, with only around 1.1% resulting in a meeting. Now for those who are concerned that such results are not worth the effort, email is still considered among B2B businesses as being able to generate up to $36 per $1 spent and with a meeting set with a senior buyer it is more than worth the effort of writing a lot more posts for months in a row!

This is then followed by something outside the scope of your email but as a result; the call will become a proposal, the proposal becomes a purchase order, the purchase order will be signed off by a person who at this stage does not know you, your company will not have existed for them as such, 5 days earlier. The salesperson will undoubtedly say that the deal was "won on the call". Were they? The reality is that it was simply the time when your email got to say out loud for the first time.

Epilogue: The Moral of the Journey

Observe what truly makes or breaks us. There was never anything about finding the perfect words. It is the infrastructure (warm domains, authentication, Google's spam-rate limit), disguise (plain text, looking like an ordinary letter), relevance of contents (inquiry over fumbling), time (a few days before end of week and early in the day) and persistence (that extra follow-up that most simply never manage to send.)
