Email Format

B2BGenerate · Guides

"Email format" covers two things, and both decide whether a message works: the format of the address itself, and the way the email is structured once it is written. This guide walks through both, from the anatomy of a business email address to the structure of a cold sales email that gets replies.

Part One: The Email Address Format

The Anatomy of a Business Email Address

Every address has the same shape: a local part, the @ symbol, and a domain. In jane.smith@acme.com, the local part is jane.smith, the domain is acme.com, and the local part usually follows a pattern the company has chosen for all its staff.

The Formats Companies Actually Use

There is no global standard, but a handful of patterns cover most of the business world:

  • first.last@domain.com, the most common in mid-size and larger companies.
  • flast@domain.com, common in smaller firms and older systems.
  • firstinitiallast@domain.com, common where duplicate names are frequent.
  • first@domain.com, common in small companies.
  • first.last@region.domain.com, used by international companies splitting mailboxes by country.

Larger companies often run two or three patterns at once, especially after acquisitions, which is why verified lookup beats guessing for anything important.

Finding the Right Format Responsibly

Patterns can be checked from public sources: company websites, published documents and professional profiles usually reveal the convention. Once a candidate address is built, it should be verified before it is used, because a wrong guess is a bounce that damages the sender's reputation. Verification tools and format-checking endpoints confirm the mailbox without sending anything.

Guessing is not outreach. A guessed address that reaches the wrong person at the right company is still a wasted send and a small dent in the sender's reputation.

Part Two: The Email Message Format

The Subject Line

Three to six words, lowercase, reading like something a colleague would send. The subject line has one job: getting the email opened without looking like marketing.

The Greeting

First name and a comma, or no greeting at all. Generic openings ("Dear Sir/Madam", "To whom it may concern") belong in the last decade.

The Body

One short paragraph connecting the sender to the recipient's situation: what you do, who it works for, and one concrete reason it matters to them now. Fifty to one hundred and fifty words total. No feature lists, no attachments on the first touch, no stacked adjectives.

The Call to Action

One low-friction ask: a short call, a yes-or-no question, or an offer to send detail. One question outperforms three.

The Signature

Real name, role, company and a working way to say no. A complete, plain signature is both a legal requirement in most markets and a trust signal.

Plain Text or HTML?

For one-to-one outreach, plain text or minimal formatting wins. Heavy HTML templates load slower, render differently in every client, and signal mass marketing to both recipients and spam filters. Marketing newsletters can carry design; a cold sales email should read like it was typed by a person.

Common Email Format Mistakes

  • Wall-of-text paragraphs that cannot be scanned on a phone.
  • Multiple asks in a single email.
  • Images and tracking pixels that trip spam filters.
  • Generic greetings copied across an entire list.
  • Signature blocks longer than the message.
  • Subject lines that promise something the body does not deliver.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the standard business email format?

Most companies use first.last@domain.com, followed by firstinitiallast@ and first@. There is no single standard, which is why formats are verified before outreach rather than guessed.

How do I find a company's email format?

Check public sources: the company website, published documents and professional profiles usually reveal the pattern. Verified lookup tools then confirm the specific mailbox before it is used.

Are guessed email addresses legal to use?

Guessing an address is not illegal by itself in most markets, but emailing it without a lawful basis can breach anti-spam rules. Verify the address and check the frameworks that apply in your market before sending.

Should cold emails be plain text or HTML?

Plain text or minimal formatting performs better for one-to-one outreach. Heavy HTML templates signal mass marketing to both recipients and spam filters.

How long should a cold sales email be?

Short. Fifty to one hundred and fifty words in short paragraphs, with one clear ask. If it can be read in under twenty seconds, it will be.

Final Thoughts

Format is not cosmetic. The right address format keeps messages deliverable, and the right message format keeps them readable. Verify before you send, keep the structure short, and make the ask easy.
