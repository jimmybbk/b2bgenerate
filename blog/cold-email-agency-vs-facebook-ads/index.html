Cold Email Agency vs Facebook Ads: Which Channel Wins for B2B Lead Generation?

B2BGenerate · Guides

If you sell a high value service to other businesses (as I do) I think you have asked yourself this problem. Where should I put my next dollar (and that is a lot of dollars) with Facebook Ads or cold email? Facebook is the largest advertising platform on earth. It is easy, intuitive and endlessly optimised. Cold email is not half as sexy but it gets you into the inbox of the person that signs the cheque.

In short, both can work. Some companies sell some products to some customers via some channels. If the objective is to arrange meetings with qualified B2B decision-makers then, most companies will be surprised at how often data and facts actually back up the argument that cold email works better than they thought it would. This piece looks in detail to compare both 2026 and then shows how to use the information to make investment decisions.

What a Cold Email Agency Actually Does

Let us start with a definition to try and distinguish "cold email" from email marketing. Email marketing is about sending out email promotions, newsletters to people on your list who have signed up. Cold email on the other hand is about outbound, about initiating conversation with people who have never heard about you, but are your ideal customer profile.

Then there is a whole agency dedicated to sending out cold email for you - this is where we come in.

  • Produce prospect lists based on the cleansed B2B databases by selecting industry, business size, revenue, country and job title criteria
  • Verification of all email addresses before using to protect the reputation of your domain.
  • Configuring and heating up of domains and inboxes to ensure all email arrives in main inbox and not spam
  • Write personalised sequences based on the actual issues facing each member of staff within their role.
  • Review the campaign on a weekly basis, ensuring that deliveries, responses and appointments take place each day and make alterations accordingly.

The result is a service that presents your offer to construction company finance directors, where the company generates between $10M and $100M, not whoever an algorithm might think is interested. We can create an individual outreach plan for you; please get in touch.

Why Facebook Ads Struggle in B2B

That said, Facebook Ads are not a bad offering; it just does not do what B2B sellers want from it, and there are four recurring themes.

1. You buy impressions (views), not leads or contacts.

Source: 1.7%-1.9% WordStream's 2026 benchmarks

No amount of brilliance in the ad (and no amount of time looking at it for all I know) will stop people scrolling it down when you are not on-screen. You will not gain a lead from an impression.

2. Target by behaviour not by department. Meta's targeting system is based more around consumer activities and interests as opposed to the traditional sales or marketing functions of the business and lookalike audiences. Trying to target customers like the CFO of a mid sized company considering invoice finance this quarter is something of an art! However it is known that the B2B audience spend more to be found; WordStream's industry benchmarks have long said the average B2B cost-per-click is at around $2.52 compared to the industry average of $1.72.

Prices go up, intent stays the same The average CPM in 2020 was around $5 but Business of Apps' $ Facebook advertising cost analysis and independent analysis of Facebook and Instagram ad spend (which is in the range of hundreds of millions in 2024) both indicate the trend for prices to keep rising. According to WordStream a typical cost across the industry for a cost per lead would be roughly around $27-$28. However B2B costs exceed this and according to Martal's cold email research they are at about $145 on Facebook and $138 on Instagram.. Even if you paid that price and you landed the contract, it is not much at all for something which could be worth $50,000. However a typical social media lead is going to be the kind of person who was waiting for something to do than an actual purchase.

4. You cannot choose when the advertising will appear. Whether it is a short and snappy purchase that you are selling or in the case of B2B products and services a longer-term decision, interruptions with no choice when or how people see your marketing will not be the best way to engage prospects.

Seven Reasons Cold Email Usually Wins for B2B

No middlemen - The person you are emailing receives it personally - no third-party auctions, feed or algorithm. It is straight from a business leader to a business leader.

Precision in your targeting. You choose which industry, how many employees and what size the company is (by revenue), what systems the business runs on and where their based. These levels of account-level targeting simply do not exist in any other type of ad account.

Sound like mail and not marketing - It is far easier to get away with sending emails that have relevant information on the organisation. A study undertaken by the RAIN Group shows that an initial meeting on a new prospect is unlikely to materialise until at least eight contacts have been made with them.

4. You do not need to build the platform you just need the pipeline. With no ad auction, creative fatigue or sudden CPM hikes at Christmas your infrastructure can be an asset not something to be afraid of.

Strong Fundamentals - The economics make this a great channel; the industry claims that $36 is made for every $1 spent (Litmus) and that is the highest ROI of any channel. Martal puts the data around what Cold Email can do in B2B - average open rates of cold campaigns around 42%, positive response rates of 4.8%, positive response rates of 1.6%, conversion to meetings of 1.1%. It is moving people away from the click to actually talking to a decision-maker.

Repeatable, modular Campaigns. Once they have been proved to work for building contractors they can simply be cloned (with slight modification) to suit logistics, manufacturing and professional services companies. Winning is exponential - campaigns do not start from scratch every month.

7. Faster, cleaner and more accurate market testing- Curious as to whether the market will react well to what you have to offer? In two weeks from targeted sending there will be much more information gleaned than a month of online advertising. If people are responding, raising problems or booking in then you have more accurate data than anything an online campaign platform can deliver in terms of 'engagement' metrics. Although, cold email does take between four to eight weeks to mature into scale, so it is definitely worth taking that on board.

To be fair, the benchmarks for cold email are low and you can take these figures with a pinch of salt. On average it costs 2%-5% to gain a response and there has been an estimate that about 300 cold emails are needed to generate a lead for B2B sales. The important point about cold emailing is that it is quality over quantity and it does not matter if you send thirty "not interested" auto-responses if you just have two proper responses.

Where Facebook Ads Genuinely Win

The flip side of fairness though! In some cases Facebook Ads are a better option than cold email.

  • You want brand awareness for a large market, such as a specific country
  • You want to retarget people who have either visited your website or interacted with your content
  • Your offer is something visual or to do with events i. E webinar, product launch, e-book.
  • Or sell low value, quickly decided products or services.
  • Speed is critical. Paid social can get you leads in days - outbound needs several weeks to build relationships and get your messaging right

Cold email is better in precision and lead quality whereas Facebook Ads are better in reach and immediacy.

The Compliance Piece Most Guides Skip

The practice is permitted (though regulated) in most of the larger markets, e.g.: U.S. (FTC's CAN-SPAM compliance guide: accurate headers/subject lines, a valid postal address and a timely opt-out response (financial penalties of up to around $46,517 can be imposed per message that breaches the law Aust. Spam Act 2003) (consent must be express or implied; sender must be identifiable; unsubscribe must be functional; the OAIC' advertising and spam guidance can provide further info), EU & UK (B2B contact tends to be governed under GDPR's legitimate-interest basis);

Ensure your provider is an experienced, trusted partner and that they have the correct processes in place as a matter of course before you discuss anything like 'guaranteed leads'. Any firm with their game face on will have more questions for you about your market, offer and sales cycle before they ever mention the word promises.

So, Which Should You Choose?

You would go for this approach if selling a high value service, ideal customer profile is well defined, it is a long sales cycle and you want the pipeline lined up with quality qualified appointments.

If however you are selling consumer or lower ticket items, wanting high turnover quickly or you need to increase awareness and retarget your audiences: go for Facebook Ads.

For many companies however, both are the answer: use Facebook to stay in touch with non-respondents and book meetings via cold email.
