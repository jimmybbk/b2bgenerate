How Much Does Cold Email Infrastructure Cost?

B2BGenerate · Guides

One of the first questions every team asks about cold outreach is what it costs to run properly. The honest answer: the emails themselves are the cheapest part. A working cold email program sits on a small stack of infrastructure that keeps messages out of spam, and that stack has real line items.

This guide itemizes them, with realistic monthly figures, so the budget is a surprise to nobody.

What Counts as Cold Email Infrastructure

  • Dedicated sending domains, separate from your main company domain.
  • Sending mailboxes on those domains.
  • Authentication: SPF, DKIM and DMARC records.
  • Warm-up tooling for new mailboxes.
  • A sequencing or sending platform.
  • List verification.
  • Prospect data.
  • Reply handling and reporting.

Sending Domains

Campaigns never send from the main company domain. They use dedicated domains, typically two to five of them, each registered for a few dollars a year. Registration costs around ten to fifteen dollars per domain annually, which is trivial next to what the domains protect: your main domain's reputation.

Mailboxes

Each sending domain needs its own mailboxes. Most campaigns run two to three mailboxes per domain, on Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 at roughly three to seven dollars per mailbox per month. A campaign with six to ten sending mailboxes costs somewhere between twenty and seventy dollars a month in mailbox fees.

Warm-Up

New mailboxes have no sending history, and mailbox providers treat them with suspicion. Warm-up tools build that history with small, gradually rising volume over two to three weeks. Warm-up tooling typically runs between nothing and thirty dollars a month depending on the provider.

The Sequencing Platform

The sending tool manages campaigns, scheduling, follow-ups and reply tracking. Plans typically run between thirty and one hundred dollars a month at campaign volumes, with the price driven by contact counts rather than features.

Verification

Every list is verified before a single email goes out. Verification is priced per email, from a fraction of a cent to around half a cent per contact at volume. A five thousand contact list costs a few dollars to verify, and it is the cheapest deliverability insurance in the stack.

Prospect Data

Sourced contact data runs from free (built manually from public research) to subscription pricing on databases, with per-contact costs on licensed enrichment. The rule: verified before it is used, whatever it cost to source.

A Working Budget Example

A single-campaign setup for a B2B team sending four to five thousand emails a month typically carries:

  • Three to five sending domains: around one to two dollars a month combined.
  • Six to ten sending mailboxes: twenty to seventy dollars a month.
  • Warm-up and verification: ten to forty dollars a month.
  • Sequencing platform: thirty to one hundred dollars a month.

That puts the tooling for a working program in the range of sixty to two hundred dollars a month, before data and before anyone's time.

The Hidden Costs

The tools are not where programs fail. The costs that matter are research hours, copywriting that sounds like a person wrote it, reply handling inside one business day, and the discipline to pause campaigns that drift. Teams that skip those pay for it in deliverability, which takes weeks to rebuild.

Build or Outsource

Building in-house suits teams who want the capability permanently and have someone to own it daily. Outsourcing suits teams who want meetings without becoming an email operations shop.

A managed program typically starts around two and a half thousand dollars a month, with infrastructure, data, copy, sending, verification and reply handling included. Performance-based programs go further: fees tied to agreed lead targets, with no fee for periods that miss them. Compare providers on those terms, not on the price of the tools alone.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I run cold email from my main company domain?

No. Cold campaigns use dedicated sending domains with their own authentication, so your main domain's reputation is never at risk. Sharing a domain between cold outreach and everyday company mail is the most common setup mistake.

How many sending domains and mailboxes do I need?

Most campaigns start with two to five sending domains and two to three mailboxes per domain. The exact setup depends on target volume and how quickly you want to ramp.

What is domain warm-up and why does it take weeks?

New domains have no sending history, so mailbox providers treat them with suspicion. Warm-up builds that history with small, gradually rising volume over two to three weeks before campaigns start.

Is it cheaper to build or outsource cold email?

Building in-house saves agency fees but adds tools, data costs and someone's time. Outsourcing makes sense when you want meetings without hiring a specialist, especially when fees are tied to agreed results.

What happens if I skip infrastructure and just send more?

Deliverability drops, bounces rise and mailbox providers start filtering your domain. Recovery takes weeks, so the shortcut usually costs more than the setup it avoided.

Final Thoughts

Cold email infrastructure is not expensive by B2B standards, but it is unforgiving to skip. Budget for the stack, protect the main domain, verify every list and measure meetings rather than sends.

Teams that respect that discipline get a channel that compounds. Teams that do not get a domain they can no longer email from.
