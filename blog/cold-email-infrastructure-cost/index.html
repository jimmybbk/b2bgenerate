How Much Does Cold Email Infrastructure Cost?

B2BGenerate · Guides

One of the first questions you will have from the rest of the team will be: what is cold outreach going to cost us to run correctly, now that we have seen it working for someone else? It is the cost of the email itself. That is the cheapest part. A successful, functioning email campaign depends on just a few line items that help get your emails out of spam. It is the running costs you would have to consider; for the actual email campaign, you do not really have to part with anything more.

They are all listed along with realistic month figures so there will be no budget surprises.

What Counts as Cold Email Infrastructure

  • Dedicated sending domains, separate from your main company domain.
  • Sending mailboxes on those domains.
  • Authentication: SPF, DKIM and DMARC records.
  • Warm-up tooling for new mailboxes.
  • A sequencing or sending platform.
  • List verification.
  • Prospect data.
  • Reply handling and reporting.

Sending Domains

Now, when campaigns send, they do not send emails from the main domain of companies. They buy domains. Two to five domains and use them. The cost of each domain would be ten to fifteen dollars yearly, which is negligible compared to protecting the main domain of your organization. Because if these purchased domains start getting blacklisted, it does not affect the main domain, and they can be blacklisted easily without affecting other operations.

Mailboxes

Each sending domain needs its own mailboxes. The typical campaign will use 2-3 mailboxes per domain. This will typically be on Google Workspace or Microsoft 365, with costing in the regions of $3-7 dollars per month per mailbox. So a campaign with six to ten sending mailboxes is going to be paying somewhere between $20-70 a month in mailbox fees.

Warm-Up

Since new mailboxes do not have a sending history, mailbox providers tend to treat new addresses with suspicion. Warm-up is the process of building that sending history and gaining the trust of the providers of the mailboxes you are using. Tools are typically zero to thirty dollars a month, depending on the service provider, typically over a two to three-week gradually increasing send volume, starting with a 0 and moving up as the addresses age.

The Sequencing Platform

Manage campaigns, schedule follow-ups, and reply-track replies through the sending tool. It is usually thirty to one hundred dollars per month plans over a certain range of number of campaigns/amount of work, with price being tied not to features but only to the amount of contacts.

Verification

A list is verified before a single email campaign goes out. Verification is handled per-email and goes from a fraction of a cent to about half a cent per contact at volume. Five thousand contacts will cost a few dollars to verify, and it is the cheapest deliverability insurance in the stack.

Prospect Data

Data of contacts can be extracted from public research (free) to subscription pricing offered by companies (based on volume of usage in contacts research - price varies anywhere between free to 5 USD per contact enrichment). The rule used here is all enriched or acquired contacts have to be cleared against the source website before reaching out.

A Working Budget Example

A single campaign setup for the B2B team delivering 4-5000 emails a month will carry a: 1) Average open rate of 12-15% with a range of 8-20% 2) Average CTR of 0.8-1.5% and a range of 0.2 to 2% (only considering those that open emails)

  • Three to five sending domains: around one to two dollars a month combined.
  • Six to ten sending mailboxes: twenty to seventy dollars a month.
  • Warm-up and verification: ten to forty dollars a month.
  • Sequencing platform: thirty to one hundred dollars a month.

To summarize, for a working database the budget for tools alone goes up to USD 60 - 200/month, exclusive of data and man hours involved.

The Hidden Costs

The problem is not the tools. The tools used do not determine if an outreach program will fail or not. The true cost of a successful outreach program is the time spent on thorough and concise research, well-written and human-sounding copywriting, replying within the deadline, and having enough will-power to pause any campaigns that divert. Companies that skip these steps eventually suffer due to deliverability scores, the rebuilding process of which can take weeks.

Build or Outsource

Teams looking for the permanent capability along with someone to own it every day will benefit with this approach as opposed to outsourcing. The latter is great for teams who want this service to have regular meetings, without having to take on email operations in their hands; this allows both the teams to excel.

A managed program will start at about two and a half thousand dollars a month and will provide infrastructure, data, copy, sending, verification and reply handling. In addition to managed programs are performance based programs: no fee for periods that miss the lead targets. You want to compare the programs, not just the price of a tool.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I run cold email from my main company domain?

No way in the world. Cold campaigns use completely separate sending domains with its own authentication, to which your domain reputation does not have any relevance. Mixing your two emailing domains (i.e., between cold outreach and normal office mail) in parallel is probably the worst set-up mistake you can make.

How many sending domains and mailboxes do I need?

They will go out with between 2-5 sending domains and 2-3 mailboxes per domain based on how much volume they need to hit and how quick they want to ramp.

What is domain warm-up and why does it take weeks?

Since new domains do not have any sending history, mailbox providers act suspiciously with such domains. Gradual building of reputation by gradually increasing the number of emails for the first few weeks will help new domains to successfully pass through anti-spam filters.

Is it cheaper to build or outsource cold email?

Building in-house avoids agency fees but involves tools/data costs and the time of someone. Outsourcing makes sense when you need to meet without hiring a specialty, and fees are usually linked to the results agreed upon.

What happens if I skip infrastructure and just send more?

The result is worse deliverability, more bounces, and in the worst-case scenarios, ISPs begin filtering from your domain; the path to redemption can take weeks, so the shortcut ends up costing more than the setup it avoided in the first place.

Final Thoughts

No - none of these things is expensive by B2B standards. Ignoring cold emails' need for dedicated infrastructure is an unforgiving mistake. Allocate budget to the stack, safeguard the master domain name, check every list and measure - not number of mails sent but meetings booked.

Teams who respect the rules create a channel that grows. Teams that do not can no longer send an email to the address that belongs to them.
