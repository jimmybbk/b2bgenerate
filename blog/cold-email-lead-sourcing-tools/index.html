Cold Email Lead Sourcing Tools: Apollo, Hunter and Yelp Restaurant Data

B2BGenerate · Guides

A cold sales email is only as good as the list behind it. Before copy is written or infrastructure is warmed up, somebody has to answer a simpler question: which companies should we be talking to, and who inside them? That answer comes from lead sourcing, and it is the step most failed campaigns skip.

This guide walks through the tools professional teams use to source B2B leads, including a worked example around restaurant data, one of the most requested verticals in food-service sales.

What Lead Sourcing Tools Actually Do

Lead sourcing turns "companies that might need us" into a verified list of named contacts. The work involves:

  • Defining the target market by industry, location and size.
  • Finding companies that match.
  • Identifying the decision-makers inside them.
  • Finding verified email addresses and phone numbers for those people.
  • Enriching each record with the details campaigns need for personalization.
  • Verifying every address before it enters a campaign.

Apollo: The All-in-One Database

Apollo combines a large contact database with filters, enrichment and sequencing. Teams filter by industry, company size, location and role, then export verified contacts with their details.

It suits campaigns that need volume across well-defined segments, and its filters make it fast to build a targeted list for a niche. Like every database, its data ages, so exports should still be re-verified before sending.

Hunter: Domain-Level Email Finding

Hunter works from the other direction: given a company's domain, it finds the email patterns and known addresses attached to it, plus checks whether those mailboxes are deliverable.

It suits teams who already have a target company list and need the right addresses, and its verification endpoint is a common way to clean a list before sending.

Public Directory Data: The Restaurant Example

For vertical campaigns, public directories are often the richest source of target companies. Restaurant data is the classic example: public listings carry the name, location, cuisine, service style, contact details and customer-review signals a campaign needs.

A restaurant equipment supplier, a commercial finance provider or a food wholesaler can build a prospect list of hundreds of venues by cuisine, postcode and size from that data alone.

One caution: directories have terms of service that restrict automated scraping, and copied data can breach platform rules even when the listing is public. Safer routes are the directory's own API, licensed data providers, or careful manual research from publicly listed business information.

From Raw Data to a Verified List

Sourced data is not sendable data. The workflow that keeps campaigns safe:

  • Deduplicate across sources.
  • Syntax-check every address.
  • Confirm the domain exists and publishes mail records.
  • Validate the mailbox where the server allows it.
  • Flag catch-all domains for conservative treatment.
  • Screen disposable and role-based addresses into their own segments.
  • Suppress opt-outs, competitors and existing contacts.

Turning Restaurant Data Into Campaigns

Once the list is clean, segmentation turns it into campaigns: fine-dining venues get a different angle from quick-service chains, and independent operators get a different offer from restaurant groups. The message stays short and specific to the segment, because relevance is the only thing that earns a reply.

Compliance Notes for Sourced Data

Business contact data can be used for relevant commercial outreach in many markets, but the frameworks differ: CAN-SPAM in the US, GDPR and PECR in Europe, CASL in Canada and Australia's Spam Act 2003. The baseline is consistent: identify the sender, use accurate data, keep the opt-out working, honor suppressions immediately and keep records of where every contact came from.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which lead sourcing tool should I start with?

Most B2B teams start with Apollo because it combines a contact database, filters and enrichment in one place, then add Hunter for domain-level email finding and a verification tool before anything is emailed.

Is scraping public directories like Yelp allowed?

Directory terms of service often restrict automated scraping, and copied data can breach platform rules even when the listing is public. Safer routes are official APIs, licensed data providers or careful manual research from publicly listed business information.

How accurate are sourced contact lists?

Data decays quickly as people change roles. Professional sourcing combines multiple sources, verifies every address before use and expects a share of contacts to need updating each quarter.

What data points matter for a restaurant prospect list?

Name, cuisine and service style, location, size indicators, ownership or group affiliation, and the operational systems already in place. Those details drive relevant messaging.

Can restaurant data be used for cold email legally?

Yes, in many markets business contact data can be used for relevant commercial outreach with identification and working opt-outs, but the rules differ: CAN-SPAM, GDPR/PECR and CASL each set their own conditions. Check the frameworks that apply before sending.

Final Thoughts

Lead sourcing is where campaigns are won. The tools make it faster, but the discipline makes it work: verified addresses, defined segments and a reason for every contact.

Build the list properly, verify it before a single send, and the rest of the campaign has a foundation worth writing copy for.
