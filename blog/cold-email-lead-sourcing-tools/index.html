Cold Email Lead Sourcing Tools: Apollo, Hunter and Yelp Restaurant Data

B2BGenerate · Guides

A good cold sales email is only so good as the list that powers it. Before there is thought given to copy or warming infrastructures, somebody has to find answers to a simpler-than-that question - 'which companies do we need to talk to?' and 'who inside these organisations?' This answer, or the lack of it, can be found in the lead sourcing process - which is a process most failed campaigns skip entirely.

This guide will take you through the tools that professional teams use for sourcing B2B leads, illustrating it with an example of real restaurant data - the most requested vertical in food-service sales.

What Lead Sourcing Tools Actually Do

Companies who "might need us" become named contacts, and they carry out the actual work:

  • Defining the target market by industry, location and size.
  • Finding companies that match.
  • Identifying the decision-makers inside them.
  • Finding verified email addresses and phone numbers for those people.
  • Enriching each record with the details campaigns need for personalization.
  • Verifying every address before it enters a campaign.

Apollo: The All-in-One Database

This combined powerful filters to search Apollo's database, contact enrichment, and prospecting sequencing. Teams could add filters such as industry, size, location, and role. After finding prospects, contacts and their details were exported, and contacts were put into a sequencing engine.

It serves campaigns with a high volume of clearly defined segments, and its filters make it fast and easy to build a relevant list for small niches. As with every database, the data still needs vetting after export and before sending, as over time, a lot of information gets out of date.

Hunter: Domain-Level Email Finding

Hunter does the other way around: you feed a domain name into it, and it returns known email addresses at that address, plus it highlights the email patterns known at the address, not forgetting to tell you IF the mailboxes are deliverable and more.

It is suitable for companies that have a list of targets and want to purchase their precise addresses, and their Verification provides an opportunity to 'clean-up' your mailing list upon delivery.

Public Directory Data: The Restaurant Example

The richest area of hunting grounds for target companies for vertical use to follow (literally) is public directories. The restaurant directory is an example; it often shows name of restaurant, location, style of cuisine, style of service, contact details, as well as providing signals on what customer reviews like to read and also can target campaigns needs directly.

Looking at only that data alone, a company selling restaurant equipment, commercial finance, or frozen prawns can come up with a list of prospects numbering in the hundreds of venues from those alone: Chinese, Italian takeaways, Thai, and Indian. The software can help break it down to restaurants (as specified in initial criteria input to search for a business) in one go in one suburb. Hence, with postcode input, one would acquire all businesses with that postcode matching business names. Such companies, in the future, can narrow down their targets with software/search engines based on criteria of the number of visitors to the venue, type of cuisines they deal with, et al., without having to do much.

Be aware that listings directories usually come with terms of service restricting the use of automated scraping tools, and copying data can be a breach of rules and practices whether or not the listed information is in the public domain. Safer ways to get hold of directory listings should include the use of the directory's own API or that of a licensed listing data provider, or a more careful, manual compilation of business' publicly available information.

From Raw Data to a Verified List

You cannot send data direct from a data source. Here is a secure workflow:

  • Deduplicate across sources.
  • Syntax-check every address.
  • Confirm the domain exists and publishes mail records.
  • Validate the mailbox where the server allows it.
  • Flag catch-all domains for conservative treatment.
  • Screen disposable and role-based addresses into their own segments.
  • Suppress opt-outs, competitors and existing contacts.

Turning Restaurant Data Into Campaigns

Having taken a little time to clean it up, segmentation transforms the prospecting list into campaigns (approaching finer-dining places from a different angle to fast-food restaurants, offering a different option to chains than independent restaurants). The message remains brief and specific to a certain segment. Only relevant offers receive responses.

Compliance Notes for Sourced Data

There are several different approaches to using business contact data for commercial outreach: use of CAN-SPAM data applies in the US, GDPR and PECR data in Europe, CASL data in Canada and Australia - through the Spam Act (2003, amended 2021 as Spam Regulations, 2022). All vary, yet follow similar baseline rules: Identify yourself, use accurate contact data, ensure the opt-out works, action all suppressions immediately, and log where all data came from.

Which lead sourcing tool should I start with?

Apollo brings together a contacts database, filters, and enrichment all in one place, so it is often where B2B teams start before adding Hunter for more extensive domain-level email finding and a verification tool prior to email.

Business contact information can and is used for relevant commercial purposes in many markets when identification is provided and opt-outs are in place; however, the laws differ across CAN-SPAM, GDPR/PECR, and CASL. You will need to find out the rules that apply to your list before using it.

Final Thoughts

Win campaigns with lead sourcing not by tools making it faster, but by the discipline making it work: verified addresses, defined segments, and a reason for every contact.

Do it properly, verify your list prior to a single send, and you will have, for the remainder of the campaign, foundations that are actually worth writing some copy about.
